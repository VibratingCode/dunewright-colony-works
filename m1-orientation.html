Welcome back.

You've been asleep a long time. Learn the site before the site teaches you.

Revival clearance

You may not work at all until Revival Med Bay clears you.

Revival Med Bay is the Stasis Revival Unit's clearance bay. It clears you to work. It doesn't treat injuries: those go to your sector's Forward Aid Station (FAS). Stasis leaves you dehydrated, weak and slow to react for the first days. You may feel fine and still fail the checks.

3Checks to pass
0Vehicles, machines or powered tools until cleared
5Heat-ramp days on the RAMP band
  • Weigh-in and hydration check. Your daily heat weigh-ins happen at the shift weigh station, against that day's starting weight, not this one.
  • Balance and vision check. Fail either and Revival Med Bay may clear you for ground-level light duty only: Deck 0, no ladders, no height, no powered tools or vehicles. The restriction goes on your badge. You're retested each day, and only Revival Med Bay lifts it.
  • Grip and reaction check. Required before you touch powered tools, cranes or vehicles.

RAMP band: a black-and-yellow chevron band reading "RAMP", with a reflective strip, worn on the front and back of your helmet. It goes on at Day 1 of your heat ramp (your first shift with any heat exposure after clearance) and comes off only after your crew lead logs Day 5 complete. Reset to Day 1, band back on, after fever, vomiting or diarrhea, any heat illness, or 7 or more days away from heat work. That applies to everyone, not only new revivals. Full ramp rules: Module 3 (HS-03).

Off work for any illness or injury? Go back to Revival Med Bay for re-clearance before your next shift.

Every grid block also has a smaller sealed, cooled shelter, call sign "Shelter [Sector]-[grid]", stocked with water, ice, toilets, a first aid kit, an AED, a radio and emergency lighting. On your first shift, find your nearest shelter, AED, FAS and muster point.

Say where you are

Every radio call, report and request uses the same location format. Medics, rescue and cranes find you by it.

Charlie-14, Tower B, Deck 3, east stair
Sector-grid

Sector Alpha to Hotel, then grid 1–40. Grid numbers are painted on every grid post.

Structure

The building or tower. Skip it on open ground.

Deck

Deck 0 is the ground. Below grade is "Deck minus 1" and so on.

Landmark

The nearest thing a stranger could find: stair, hatch, haul road, end of a trench.

Open ground: "Delta-22, Deck 0, haul road east". Trench: "Echo-7, trench, north end". Say grid numbers digit by digit: "Charlie one-four".

Radio rules

  • Scan rule: every radio scans Ch 1 Site Emergency, Ch 16 Atmospheric/Weather and your sector channel. Crane, equipment and hot-work crews add their trade channel.
  • Routine crew traffic and check-ins go on your foreman talkgroup (for example "C-F14"). That's what "crew channel" means.
  • Ch 1 is for emergencies only. No chatter, no checks. Hub Command rebroadcasts every siren and every sandstorm, flood and lightning warning on Ch 1.
  • Buddy pairs always. Buddies watch each other the whole shift. At Extreme caution heat level and above, stay within sight of your buddy. Your crew lead sees every crew member at least every 30 minutes.
  • Full channel plan, call signs and check-in rules: radio reference (REF-RAD).

Emergency call · Ch 1

"Emergency, emergency, emergency." Then: type · location · number hurt · hazards · your call sign.

Heat casualty · Man down · Fall · Fire · Gas · Chemical · Structural · Struck-by · Caught-in · Electrical · Confined space · Flood · Sandstorm

A short qualifier is fine ("Fall, worker suspended"). A burn with nothing still burning is "Man down".

After the call, stay on Ch 1 until Hub Command or the medic releases you. Everyone else stays off Ch 1. Other channels keep working.

ALL STOP ("ALL STOP, ALL STOP" on any channel, or 3 short siren blasts): stop all motion, hot work and machine operation. Hold position. A suspended load is landed only on the signal person's direction. Only whoever called it, or command, clears it, on the same channel.

First shift

In this order · Your crew lead signs each step
  1. Pass revival clearance at Revival Med Bay. Collect your badge, radio and RAMP band.
  2. Draw your kit from the Quartermaster (list below). Respirator: medical evaluation first (Revival Med Bay must clear you to wear one), then training and your fit test with Industrial Hygiene. See Module 2 (HS-02).
  3. Report to your crew lead at the assigned muster point of the sector you're working in today. Get your foreman talkgroup and your buddy.
  4. Walk the route with your buddy: nearest FAS, shelter, AED, cooling station, toilets, drinking water and muster point.
  5. Radio check on your foreman talkgroup. Confirm your scan list: Ch 1, Ch 16 and your sector channel (plus your trade channel if you have one).
  6. Finish HS-01, HS-02, HS-03 and HS-09 before you set foot on a work face. Modules are posted at every muster point and on the refresher index.
  7. Day 1 of your heat ramp: no more than 20% of the shift in the heat, light work only (no heavy lifting, digging or hauling), and half the work/rest table's work minutes. Spend the rest of the shift out of the heat: refresher modules, fit testing, kit issue. Your crew lead logs your minutes in the heat.

Completion rule

  • Before Day 1 at any work face: HS-01, HS-02, HS-03 and HS-09.
  • Before your first 100% shift: all nine modules and all four supplements, Supplement A (HS-SA) to Supplement D (HS-SD).
  • Before any task: that task's module or supplement, and the badge endorsement for it.

Chain of command

Crew of about 12 → Crew Lead → Foreman → General Foreman → Sector Superintendent → Deputy Director → Colony Construction Director.

Go to your crew lead first. Safety concerns can go straight to any green hat.

At a scene: the first person there is in charge until relieved, and hands over command out loud. Fire & Rescue takes command of fire, rescue and hazmat scenes when it arrives. The Super runs muster and headcount.

Full chart: org chart (REF-ORG). Role names: roles and glossary (REF-GL).

Badge rules

  • Wear it on your chest, photo out, every shift.
  • It lists your completed modules, supplements and endorsements (machines, permits, roles), your fit-test date and your ramp status. No badge entry, no task.
  • Pre-stasis qualifications count only after you pass a practical test here.
  • Never lend it. Never badge someone else through a gate.
  • Lost badge: tell your crew lead the same shift. Your crew lead reports it to the Security Desk.

Kit issue

From the Quartermaster (Ch 15):

  • Helmet, boots, gloves, eye and hearing protection.
  • Hi-vis: Class 2 by day, Class 3 at night, or a hi-vis cooling vest.
  • Cooling vest.
  • Personal lock (red, engraved with your Colonist ID) and tags.
  • Each crew carries a trauma kit (two tourniquets) and a first aid kit.
  • Respirator only after your respirator medical evaluation and fit test.

Report it

Report every injury, illness, first aid case, near miss, damage and unsafe condition to your crew lead before the end of the shift. Anything serious: immediately. Use form HS-IR with your Colonist ID.

  • Don't move anything except to rescue someone.
  • Safety & Health investigates with the crew. Findings are shared at muster.
  • No retaliation for reporting. A near miss is an accident that didn't happen this time. The next crew depends on what you tell us.
  • Someone needs a medic now? Ch 1 first, then report.

Site services

  • Toilets and drinking water within a short walk of every deck. Never drink less to avoid a long walk.
  • Drinking water taps are blue-labeled "DRINKING WATER". Recycled water and coolant never are. Never drink from an unlabeled tap.
  • Cooling station with an ice trough on every deck of active structures. It's also your shift weigh station.
  • First aid: every crew has a first aider (CPR and Stop the Bleed). Know who yours is.
  • Visitors wear purple hats and are always escorted. Their escort keeps them in PPE and in the headcount.
  • Sand stingers: venomous burrowing arthropods shelter in rock, debris and stored gear. Shake out boots, gloves and PPE before you put them on. Look before you reach into gaps. Report any sting to the FAS.

Your body after stasis

Stasis costs you muscle. Strength comes back over weeks, not days.

  • Team lift or a mechanical aid (hoist, cart, telehandler) for anything heavy, awkward, or carried across loose sand.
  • One-person lift limit: 50 lb in good conditions (site rule). Go lower when the load is low, far from your body, twisting or repeated.
  • Ramp days and light duty: 25 lb (site rule).
  • Keep the load close and turn with your feet, not your back. Panels in wind act like sails: carry them in pairs.
  • Heavy work in heat can break down muscle (rhabdomyolysis). Dark urine, or muscle pain or weakness: go to the FAS now (HS-03).

Your mind after stasis

Disorientation, memory gaps, poor sleep, low mood and grief for lost time and people are common after stasis.

  • Talk to someone. Stasis Revival Unit (SRU) counselors at Revival Med Bay: walk in, any shift. Your FAS medic can refer you to Central Med counselors at the Hub.
  • No penalty for asking for help. What you say stays confidential unless someone's safety is at risk.
  • Worried about a coworker? Tell your crew lead or the FAS. If someone talks about harming themselves, stay with them and get your crew lead to take them to an SRU counselor or the FAS. Immediate danger: call Ch 1 "Man down". Never broadcast the reason.

Lone work and fitness for duty

  • No lone work at Caution heat level or higher, at height, in confined spaces, or near wadis and trenches. Anywhere else, lone work only with 15-minute check-ins and a logged location.
  • Come to work fit: rested, sober and well. Too tired to stay alert, or unwell? Tell your crew lead before you start. On a medicine that makes you drowsy? Tell your FAS before your next shift; the medic decides what work you can do. No penalty for saying so.
  • Fever, vomiting or diarrhea: go to the FAS. It resets your heat ramp to Day 1.
  • Night work, shift limits, fatigue and fitness-for-duty rules: Supplement D (HS-SD).

Stop-work authority

Anyone can stop unsafe work. No penalty, no questions. Stop it, make it safe, tell your crew lead (your foreman if there's no crew lead; any green hat if it isn't fixed). Work restarts only when the crew lead confirms the fix with the person who stopped it. Every stop is logged.
