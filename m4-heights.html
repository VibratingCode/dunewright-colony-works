Tie off.

Falls kill more construction workers than anything else. A city means towers. Every deck above zero is a fall.

The 6-foot rule

At 6 feet, you need fall protection.

Any edge, hole or opening where you could fall 6 feet or more to a lower level needs a guardrail, a safety net, or a personal fall arrest system. Deck 1 and above always counts (site rule). At Deck 0, the edges of pits, shafts and trenches 6 ft deep count too. Rebar, starter bars or moving machinery below you get capped or guarded at any height.

6 ft for every trade, including steel erection, connecting and decking (site rule). Old Earth let ironworkers go to 15 ft and connectors to 30 ft. Here, 100% tie-off starts at 6 ft: you are protected every second, including while you move. Moving along steel? Use a twin-leg lanyard or an SRL, and clip the second leg before you unclip the first.

Guardrails

Protects everyone, every time
  • Top rail 42 inches above the walking surface, plus or minus 3 inches.
  • Top rail holds 200 lb pushed out or down, and stays at 39 in or higher under that push.
  • Midrail about halfway (around 21 inches), or mesh or screen that fills the gap. Midrails and screens hold 150 lb. No opening bigger than 19 in.
  • Toe board at least 3.5 inches tall, no more than ¼ in off the floor, holds 50 lb. Fit one wherever tools or material can be kicked off the edge.
  • Wire rope top rail: flag it every 6 ft.
  • Guardrail section out to land a load? Everyone in that hoist area ties off. When no load is coming in, a chain or gate closes the gap. Put the rail back when the landing is done.
  • Never lean on, sit on or climb a guardrail. Never hang a load or a lanyard from one.

Fall clearance

Know how far you'll drop before you clip in
18.5 ftExample: clearance below the anchor, 6 ft lanyard
6 ft
Free fall: how far you drop before the lanyard starts to stop you. Anchor at your D-ring: free fall = lanyard length. 6 ft is the most allowed. Anchor overhead to cut it.
3.5 ft
Deceleration: how far the shock pack tears open while it stops you. 3.5 ft is the Old Earth maximum (OSHA). ANSI Z359.13 packs can open up to 4 ft. Use the number on your lanyard's label.
1 ft
Harness stretch: the webbing stretches and the D-ring slides up your back.
5 ft
D-ring to feet: your body hangs below the D-ring.
3 ft
Safety margin: space left between your boots and the next deck, beam or obstruction.
  • Add it up from the anchor: 6 + 3.5 + 1 + 5 + 3 = 18.5 ft. Less than that below the anchor? A 6 ft shock-absorbing lanyard hits the deck or steel before it stops you.
  • Short on clearance? Use a self-retracting lifeline (SRL) anchored overhead. An SRL still needs clearance. Check its label.
  • Anchor at your feet, or working a leading edge (decking, forming, slab and roof edges)? Use only an SRL rated for leading edges (marked LE), and add the extra clearance on its label.
  • No controlled access zones or safety monitors in place of fall protection (site rule).
  • Swing fall: an anchor off to the side swings you into structure. Anchor overhead, as close above you as you can.

Anchors

  • 5,000 lb per worker attached, or engineered by a qualified person (Design & Structures) with a 2:1 safety factor for the whole system.
  • Never anchor to guardrails, conduit, pipe or a load.
  • Your lifeline anchor is never the anchor that holds a swing stage or platform.
  • Not sure an anchor is rated? Don't use it. Ask the competent person for fall protection.

Fall arrested

Hanging in a harness cuts off blood flow from the legs. Suspension trauma can make a conscious worker pass out in minutes and can kill.

No rescue plan,
no climb.

Plan to reach a hanging worker within 6 minutes (ANSI Z359.2). Waiting for Fire & Rescue from the Hub or a sector team takes too long. Your crew does the rescue.

  1. Ch 1: "Emergency, emergency, emergency. Fall, worker suspended." Then location, number hurt, hazards, your call sign: "Charlie-14, Tower B, Deck 3, east stair. One hurt. Hanging over open steel. Charlie Crew 14-3 Seven." Stay on Ch 1 until Hub Command or the medic releases you.
  2. Talk to them. Keep them calm and moving their legs. Have them stand in their relief straps or push against the structure.
  3. Run the rescue plan now with the deck's fall-rescue kit. Rescuers clip in before they reach out. Don't wait for medics.
  4. Once down, lay them flat. Unconscious but breathing: recovery position. Treat as a trauma patient. Watch breathing. Not breathing normally: first aider starts CPR and someone brings the AED from the cooling station.
  5. Everyone who hung in a harness goes to the Forward Aid Station (FAS), even if they feel fine.
  6. Take the harness, lanyard or SRL out of service. Tag it. Safety & Health inspects it.

Rescue plan & kits

  • Every job at height has a written rescue plan before anyone goes up: the method, the named rescuers and the gear. Your crew lead and the competent person for fall protection make it. It's posted at the job (site rule).
  • Fall-rescue kits at every structure, kept on each active deck beside the cooling station (site rule): a pre-rigged haul-and-lower system, anchor slings and a reach pole.
  • Rescuers are trained on that kit. Know where yours is before you clip in.
  • Kit seal broken or gear missing? Nobody climbs on that deck until it's restocked.

Harness check, every use

  • Webbing: no cuts, frays, burns, weld spatter holes, chemical stains, or hard, glazed spots. Faded, stiff or powdery webbing = out. Sun and sand wear gear fast.
  • Stitching: no pulled or broken threads.
  • Hardware: D-rings and buckles not bent, cracked or pitted. Snap hooks lock and close by themselves.
  • Lanyard or SRL: shock pack intact. Torn pack or red indicator showing = it already caught a fall. SRL line pulls out and locks.
  • Heat rating: every harness, lanyard and SRL label shows a temperature range. DCW issues desert-rated gear. Check the max °F on yours. Air or steel hotter than the label? Don't use it. Switch to hot-rated gear or stop work. Store gear in cooled lockers. Keep lines off hot steel edges.
  • Capacity: standard gear holds 130–310 lb: you, tools and clothes. Heavier? Draw heavy-rated gear.
  • Connecting: never clip two snap hooks together, or two into one D-ring unless both are rated for it. Park the spare leg of a twin lanyard on the lanyard keeper. Never on a D-ring.
  • Fit: chest strap mid-chest, leg straps snug enough for a flat hand under them, D-ring between your shoulder blades.
  • The competent person for fall protection inspects every harness and lanyard at least yearly, more often here. Check the inspection label.
  • Any doubt: tag it out. Draw a new one from the Quartermaster.

Ladders

4:1Up 4, out 1
3 ftAbove landing
3Points of contact
  • Set the base 1 foot out for every 4 feet up. Extend the rails 3 feet above the landing, and tie the top off.
  • Never set a ladder straight on loose sand. Use a base board or ladder mat. Someone foots it until the top is tied.
  • Check it before each use: rails, rungs, feet, locks. Bent, cracked, or fiberglass showing fibers or a chalky bloom = red tag.
  • Type IA (300 lb) or heavier. That rating covers you plus tools and gear.
  • Always 2 hands and a foot, or 2 feet and a hand. Face the ladder. Carry tools on your belt or haul them up. Never stand on the top two rungs of a stepladder.
  • Metal ladders conduct and get hot. Fiberglass near power. Gloves on sun-baked rungs.

Holes

  • A hole is any gap 2 in or more across in a floor, roof or deck.
  • Every hole gets a cover or a guardrail.
  • Covers hold twice the heaviest load that could cross them: workers, equipment, material. Where vehicles run: twice the axle load of the heaviest vehicle.
  • Secured so wind, equipment or feet can't shift them. Marked "HOLE" or "COVER".
  • Sand buries paint. After a sandstorm, check every marking, and add a raised or tagged marker.
  • Need a cover off? Only with your crew lead's OK, with the hole guarded or you tied off. Put it back.
  • Never lift a cover you didn't set.

Dropped objects

  • A wrench dropped from Deck 10 can kill someone at Deck 0.
  • Tool lanyards on every hand tool used at height. Tools over 5 lb tether to the structure. Never to your wrist or belt.
  • Toe boards, screens or netting on open edges.
  • Drop zone below overhead work: roped off at least 10 ft out from the edge, wider for higher decks and in wind, with signs on every side (site rule). Nobody walks through it.
  • Chin strap on above Deck 0 (site rule).
  • Never throw anything down. Lower it or use a chute.

Housekeeping

  • Sand on steel decks rolls like ball bearings. Wet-sweep decks and stairs every shift. Never dry-sweep (HS-02).
  • Cords and hoses run overhead or along the edge of the walkway. Never across stairs, ladders or landings. Cord rules: Supplement B (HS-SB).
  • No stored material within 6 ft of a floor opening or hoistway, or within 10 ft of an open outside edge.
  • Debris goes down a chute or in a lowered bin.
  • End of shift: walkways clear, loose material tied down.

Lighting at height

  • Lights fail while you're up: stop and hold. Stay clipped in. Don't move until the lights are back, or your crew lead brings light and guides you down.
  • Ladders, stairs, holes, edges and landing zones are lit before night work starts.
  • Dark spot or failed light? Tell your crew lead before work goes on there.
  • Light levels and night-shift rules: Supplement D (HS-SD).

Steel erection & temporary works

  • 100% tie-off at 6 ft for every trade, including connectors and decking crews (site rule).
  • Columns stand on at least 4 anchor rods before steel goes on them.
  • Bolt before you release the load line: at least 2 bolts per connection, wrench-tight, before the hook lets go.
  • Temporary bracing, guys and shoring go in as the structure goes up. They stay until the permanent structure takes the load.
  • Design & Structures approves all temporary works (bracing, shoring, formwork, temporary supports) and when they come out.
  • Printed walls (FerroLattice-7) carry no load, bracing or anchor until their cure time is up and Design & Structures releases them.
  • Something cracks, sags, shifts or groans? Get everyone off it. Ch 1: "Emergency, emergency, emergency. Structural."

Who's who at height

  • Competent person (fall protection): a trained worker named by your foreman, able to spot fall hazards, with authority to stop work. Badge endorsement. Checks anchors, gear, kits and the rescue plan.
  • Qualified person: has the training or proven skill to solve the problem, for example a Design & Structures engineer who designs an anchor or temporary works.
  • Lift Director (white hat): Crane & Lift supervisor who approves lift plans prepared by Design & Structures and directs critical lifts.
  • Qualified signal person and qualified rigger (orange hats): run every load landed on your deck. Badge endorsements.
  • Crew lead: names buddies, rescuers and the rescue method before anyone goes up.
  • Full list: REF-GL roles & glossary.

ALL STOP

  • "ALL STOP, ALL STOP" on any channel, or 3 short siren blasts: stop all motion, hot work and machine operation.
  • Hold position. At height, stay clipped in where you are.
  • A suspended load is landed only on the signal person's direction.
  • Cleared on the same channel by whoever called it, or by command. Nothing moves until you hear it cleared.

Loads landed on your deck

  • Only the qualified signal person signals the operator. Anyone can give STOP.
  • Stay out of the fall zone: under the load and anywhere it could land if it drops or swings. Hands on the load only once it's lowered to working height and steady.
  • Radio lost during a lift: the operator stops all motion and holds the load. Resume only when the radio is back and tested, or when the signal person is in the operator's full, continuous view and the operator, signal person and whoever directs the lift (the Lift Director, or the lift lead on a routine lift) agree to switch to hand signals. Blind lifts stay stopped.
  • Lift rules: HS-06. Signals: REF-HS.

Wind table (site rule)

Wind = the higher of the local anemometer (crane, MEWP or crew meter) and the Ch 16 posting. Gust = 3-second gust. Wind is stronger high up: at height, read the crew meter at your work spot.

Lightning alert

Strikes within 10 miles. Booms down, off steel, out of MEWPs, into sealed shelters or closed vehicles (never under steel). Resume 30 minutes after the last strike within 10 miles (NWS 30-minute rule; the 10-mile trigger is a site rule).

At height: stay clipped in as you move, come down by the stair or ladder, leave the tools.

Sandstorm

  • Watch (possible within 2 hours): secure loose material, plan how loads come down, stage crews near shelter.
  • Warning (arrival within 30 minutes): land all loads, booms down, climbers and MEWPs down, move to shelter.
  • The take-shelter siren sounds no later than 15 minutes before arrival. Start down when the warning is called. Don't wait for the siren.

Sirens

  • Take shelter: steady tone for 3 minutes, repeated every 5. Red strobes. Clip in, make safe, climb down, go to shelter.
  • Evacuate: alternating high-low tone for 3 minutes, repeated. Amber strobes. Climb down, go to your muster point.
  • All stop: 3 short blasts. Hold position.
  • All clear: one 15-second tone, then Hub Command on Ch 1: "All clear, all clear, [area]." Never leave a shelter or restart work on a tone alone.

Heat and height stack up

A harness traps heat against your body. Steel decks and beams pass 158°F in direct sun and burn skin in about a second. Heat dizziness at Deck 0 is a stumble. At Deck 12 it's a fall.

  • Gloves and full coverage for any work on sun-baked steel. Kneel on a pad.
  • Water, shade and a cooling station with an ice trough on the deck you're working on. Never 12 decks down.
  • Dizzy, cramping or confused? Clip in, sit down, call your buddy. Come down with help (HS-03).
  • Climbing counts as heavy work on the DCW work/rest table.

Stop-work authority

Anyone can stop unsafe work. No penalty, no questions. Stop it, make it safe, tell your crew lead (your foreman if there's no crew lead; any green hat if it isn't fixed). Work restarts only when the crew lead confirms the fix with the person who stopped it. Every stop is logged. Stop it when someone is at an edge without protection, a guardrail is down, a hole is open, or there is no rescue plan.

Report it

Report every injury, illness, first aid case, near miss, damage and unsafe condition to your crew lead before the end of the shift (immediately for anything serious), on form HS-IR with your Colonist ID. Don't move anything except to rescue. Safety & Health investigates with the crew; findings are shared at muster. No retaliation. Every arrested fall and every dropped object counts.

Worker acknowledgement

I know the 6-foot rule and 100% tie-off, I can inspect and fit my harness, I know how to figure fall clearance, I know where the fall-rescue kit is, and I will not climb without a rescue plan.
