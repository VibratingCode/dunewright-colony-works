Trades in every sector

  • Steel & ironwork: erection, bolting, decking
  • Structural print & concrete: printed walls, pours, finishing
  • Framing & panels: hab shells, cladding, seals
  • Site services: trenching, utilities, pipe
  • Finishing & fit-out: interiors, hatches, fixtures
  • General labor: laydown, cleanup, water runs, cooling stations

Cranes, heavy equipment, fabrication, polymers and power are site-wide departments. They're assigned into sectors job by job, take direction from the sector on site, and muster with the sector they're working in that day.

Muster and command

  • Muster at the assigned muster point of the sector you're working in today (two per sector, MP-A1 to MP-H2).
  • A muster warden (a named crew lead at each MP) collects crew headcounts and reports once to the Super on the sector channel. Crew leads also tell their own department where their people are.
  • Shelters: the senior person reports the headcount on that shelter's sector channel. Missing persons go on Ch 1.
  • At a scene: the first person there is incident commander until relieved, and hands over command out loud. Fire & Rescue takes command of fire, rescue and hazmat scenes when it arrives. The Super runs muster and headcount.

Rescue and first aid capacity

Fire & Rescue is 240 people across every shift: Hub HQ plus a team in each sector. They can't stand at every job, so rescue is planned before work starts.

  • Standby: when a permit names a Fire & Rescue standby (for example a Supplement A (HS-SA) confined-space entry), the team is at the job and briefed before work starts. No standby on site, no start.
  • On call: everywhere else, the sector team responds from its base. Targets (site rule): Fire & Rescue on scene within 10 minutes; confined-space on-call rescue at the hatch and starting rescue within 10 minutes. Your crew starts the rescue it's trained for: the deck's fall-rescue kit (HS-04), retrieval systems at entries (HS-SA), first aid.
  • Every crew has a first aider (CPR and Stop the Bleed), a trauma kit with two tourniquets and a first aid kit. An AED is never more than a 3-minute round trip away.
  • Medics: 120 Forward Aid Station medics across 8 sectors and all shifts. Target (site rule): a FAS medic on scene within 4 minutes anywhere in the sector. Central Med takes serious cases.
