Never under the load.

Cranes, rigging, heavy equipment, traffic and trenches. Crush and struck-by deaths happen in a second.

Rule zero

Never stand, walk or work under a suspended load.

Slings snap, hooks fail, and loads swing in gusts. If a load is in the air, you stay outside its fall zone: the area under the load and anywhere it could land if it drops or swings. If the load has to pass over you, you leave. Operators: never swing a load over people. Stop and wait for the area to clear.

Every lift, in order

Each lift on its own talkgroup · Ch 11 = coordination only
  1. Plan the lift. Every lift has a lift plan that names its lift talkgroup. Load weight = load + hook block + slings, shackles and spreader bars + anything hanging + the chart's deductions (for example a stowed jib). Check radius, boom length and capacity at that radius. Critical lift? It needs a critical lift plan (LP-) prepared by Design & Structures and approved by the Lift Director.
  2. Inspect. The operator, or another competent person, inspects the crane before every shift it runs. Crane & Lift does and logs the monthly and annual inspections: check the cab log. No current annual = no lift. Riggers inspect every sling, shackle and hook before use. Damaged gear gets tagged out and removed.
  3. Set up on solid ground. Loose sand won't hold an outrigger. A qualified person checks the ground and sizes the crane mats for the outrigger loads; loose sand usually needs engineered mats. Full outrigger extension. Level within the maker's limit (usually 1%). Check for buried services (PTW-DG), voids and fresh backfill.
  4. Barricade the swing radius. Hard barricade around the rotating counterweight so no one gets crushed between it and anything else.
  5. Set up radios and signals. Test radios on the lift talkgroup. The operator's radio is hands-free and does not scan. Assign one qualified signal person, in the operator's view or on the talkgroup. Brief the crew: the lift lead, load path, landing zone, who signals, and who keeps Ch 1 and Ch 16 (named on the lift plan, never the operator).
  6. Check wind and load heat. Wind against the table below. IR-check the load temperature before you rig (sling heat limits below).
  7. Test lift. Lift a few inches, hold, check brakes, balance and rigging. Set it down and fix anything that moves.
  8. Lift with taglines. Dry, nonconductive taglines, long enough to keep you outside the fall zone. Never wrap one around your hand or body; let go if the load pulls. Never hold a load with your hands, and never ride the hook. Connectors take steel only once it's lowered to working height and steady: hands on the member, body never under it.

Lift radio

  • Every lift gets its own lift talkgroup, named on the lift plan (for example "Lift C-07").
  • Ch 11 is coordination only, never for moving loads.
  • During a lift the operator's radio is hands-free, tested before the lift, and does not scan.
  • Every signal starts with the crane's call sign: "Crane Charlie 7, hoist, ten feet, slow ... five ... two ... stop."
  • The lift plan names who keeps Ch 1 and Ch 16 on scan for the lift crew and passes warnings on the talkgroup. Never the operator (site rule). Sirens and cab strobes still reach the operator.

Radio lost mid-lift

  • The operator stops all motion and holds the load.
  • Resume only when the radio is back and tested, or when the signal person is in the operator's full, continuous view and the operator, signal person and whoever directs the lift (the Lift Director, or the lift lead on a routine lift) agree to switch to hand signals.
  • Blind lifts stay stopped.
  • At night, hand signals need lighted wands or gloves.
  • Site hand signals (evacuate, take shelter) are for people, never for operators. Signal person: give EMERGENCY STOP first, then the site signal once the load is held.

ALL STOP

  • "ALL STOP, ALL STOP" on any channel, or 3 short siren blasts: stop all motion, hot work and machine operation.
  • Hold position. A suspended load is landed only on the signal person's direction.
  • Cleared on the same channel by whoever called it, or by command.
  • Emergency stop by hand: both arms out, palms down, swing them back and forth level with your shoulders. Every operator obeys it, whoever gives it.

Critical lifts

Any of these makes a lift critical. It needs a critical lift plan (LP-) prepared by Design & Structures and approved by the Lift Director, a pre-lift meeting, and the Lift Director directing the lift.

  • 75% or more of chart capacity at that radius
  • Two or more cranes on one load
  • Personnel lifts
  • Over occupied structures (habs count)
  • Unusual loads: high sail area, unknown weight, odd center of gravity

Personnel lifts

  • People ride a crane only when stairs, ladders, a MEWP or a scaffold would be more dangerous or impossible.
  • Rated personnel platform, proof-tested to 125% of its rating at each site.
  • Trial lift with the empty, weighted platform before every shift that lifts people.
  • Pre-lift meeting. Everyone harnessed and clipped to the platform's anchor. Operator at the controls the whole time.
  • Old Earth lets a qualified person decide above 20 mph. Here: no crane personnel platforms at 20+ mph (site rule).

Tower & overlapping cranes

  • Tower cranes follow their maker's manual and ASME B30.3. Out of service, the jib is set to free-slew (weathervane) per the maker.
  • Cranes whose swing areas overlap work to a written plan with zones or an anti-collision system. Operators coordinate on Ch 11.
  • Telehandlers lifting suspended loads follow the crane rules on this page.

Power lines & site grids

Site power grids, bus bars and overhead conduits count

Lifting near lines

Minimum distance between any part of the crane, load or rigging and an energized line.

VoltageMinimum clearance
Up to 350 kV20 ft
350 – 1,000 kV50 ft
Over 1,000 kVSet by Electrical & Power and a registered engineer (Old Earth rule)
  • Electrical & Power marks every line's voltage before the lift. Voltage not marked? No lift (site rule).
  • Could any part of the crane, load or rigging get inside that distance at full working radius? Set a raised warning line or barricade at that distance, plus a dedicated spotter or a proximity alarm or range limiter. Use dry, nonconductive taglines.
  • Can't stay out? Electrical & Power de-energizes the line, locks it out and visibly grounds it at the work spot, then confirms to the operator. Otherwise, no lift.

Wind table (site rule)

Wind = the higher of the local anemometer (crane, MEWP or crew meter) and the Ch 16 posting. Gust = 3-second gust.

WindRule
15+ mphGoggles outside cabs. Secure sheet goods and loose material.
20+ mph sustained or gustNo outdoor hot work. No crane personnel platforms. High-sail loads need Lift Director approval.
25 mph sustained or 30 mph gustOff open steel, roofs, leading edges, swing stages and outdoor scaffolds above Deck 1. MEWPs down (or the maker's limit if lower).
30 mph sustained or gustAll crane loads landed; booms secured per maker.
Blowing-dust advisory (visibility 1 mile or less)No outdoor hot work. Lifts stop whenever the operator loses sight of the load or signal person.
  • Crane wind is boom-tip wind. Maker limits apply at the highest point of the boom or jib, as a 3-second gust. The cab reads low. Use the boom-tip anemometer.
  • No working boom-tip anemometer? That crane doesn't lift at 20 mph or more on the Ch 16 posting (site rule).
  • The crane maker's limit is lower than this table? The maker's limit wins.

Sirens & horns

  • Site sirens come from the tower speakers, are repeated on Ch 1, and flash strobes in sealed cabs.
  • Take shelter: steady tone for 3 minutes, repeated every 5. Red strobes. Evacuate: alternating high-low tone for 3 minutes, repeated. Amber strobes.
  • All stop: 3 short siren blasts (1 second each).
  • All clear: one 15-second tone, then Hub Command by voice on Ch 1. Never restart work on a tone alone.
1Stopping
2Moving forward
3Backing up

Machine horns are about that machine only. They are never site signals. Operators sound the horn before moving. Hear 3 horn blasts from a machine near you? Get out of its path. Don't freeze.

Rigging

No tag, no lift

Reject it if…

  • No tag or unreadable capacity tag.
  • Crane wire rope: 6-strand running rope with 6 broken wires in one lay, or 3 in one strand in one lay. Rotation-resistant rope (most hoist lines): 2 broken wires in 6 rope diameters, or 4 in 30. Standing ropes and pendants: more than 2 broken wires in one lay, or more than 1 at an end fitting.
  • Any rope: worn more than 5% under nominal diameter, a broken strand, core sticking out, kinks, birdcaging, crushing, heavy corrosion, heat or arc damage, or any power-line contact.
  • Wire rope slings: 10 broken wires in one lay, or 5 in one strand in one lay.
  • Chain slings: alloy (grade 80 or 100) only, with a tag. Out for stretched, nicked, gouged or cracked links, or heat discoloration.
  • Synthetic slings: cuts, burns, melted or charred spots, exposed red core yarns, UV-faded and stiff. This sun eats synthetics.
  • Hooks: throat opened more than 5% (¼ in max), any visible bend or twist, worn 10% or more, any crack, latch missing or broken.
  • Shackles: wrong pin, bent body, pin not fully seated.

Heat & sharp edges

  • IR-check the load before you rig. Riggers carry an IR thermometer (site rule). Sun-baked steel passes 158°F, and dark loads at Extreme heat level go well past 194°F.
  • Synthetic slings (nylon, polyester): never on a load or in air over 194°F, lower if the tag says. HMPE slings: the tag's limit.
  • Fiber-core wire rope: never over 180°F.
  • Hotter than that? Use alloy chain or IWRC wire rope slings.
  • Store slings in cooled or shaded racks. Never on hot steel.
  • Sharp edges (beam flange, plate, hull plate, printed corner): rated edge protectors every time. A synthetic sling on a bare steel edge can cut through mid-lift.

Sling angle eats capacity

The flatter the sling, the harder each leg pulls. Multiply each leg's share of the load by the factor.

Angle from horizontalLoad factor per leg
90° (straight up)1.00 ×
60°1.15 ×
45°1.41 ×
30°2.00 ×

Example: a 10,000 lb panel on two legs at 30°. Each leg carries 5,000 × 2 = 10,000 lb. Don't rig flatter than 30°.

Three- or four-leg bridle? Count only 2 legs as carrying the whole load, unless a qualified rigger or engineer works it out. Load off center? The legs nearer the center of gravity carry more. Get the rigger to calculate it.

Lifting magnets & field rigs

  • Nobody in the fall zone, ever. Never over people.
  • Before every lift: the power-loss alarm and backup power work.
  • Clean contact face. Sand, rust, paint and thin or curved plate cut holding power. Check the magnet's chart and test-lift a few inches.
  • Steel only. Never gas cylinders, bundles or loose stacks.
  • Shutting down: land the load first, then dump the coils and verify the field is at or below the rig's posted safe level (HS-07).

Do

  • Know who the signal person is, and the lift talkgroup, before the load moves.
  • Give STOP the moment you see danger. Anyone can give it. The operator obeys it every time.
  • Stay outside barricades and the counterweight swing.
  • Use taglines and stand clear of pinch points when landing.
  • Learn the hand signals.

Don't

  • Walk under a load, ever.
  • Ride a hook, load or headache ball.
  • Take signals from more than one person (except STOP).
  • Side-load or drag a load with the crane.
  • Climb on a crane that's running or about to swing.
  • Move a load on Ch 11.

Heavy equipment

Heavy Equipment Pool runs on Ch 12

If you can't see the operator's eyes, they can't see you.

Make eye contact and get a wave before you walk near any machine. Approach from the cab side, in their view.

  • Walk-around inspection before every shift: tires, tracks, leaks, lights, alarm, mirrors, cameras, fire extinguisher.
  • Three points of contact getting on and off. Face the machine. Never jump down.
  • Seat belt on, every time. The rollover cage (ROPS) only protects you if you stay inside it.
  • Backup alarm working, and a spotter whenever you back up somewhere you can't see.
  • Loose sand gives way. Stay off soft edges, dune faces and fresh fill. Drive straight up and down slopes, never across.
  • Lower the bucket or blade, set the brake and shut down before you leave the seat.
  • Never stand between a machine and a fixed object, or between two machines. Stay out of an excavator's swing.

Traffic management

  • Traffic plan: each sector posts its haul loops, pedestrian routes, crossings, parking and fuel points. Follow the signs. Haul loops run one way where they can.
  • Speed: the posted limit. Nothing posted: 15 mph on haul loops, 5 mph in yards, laydown and near people (site rule).
  • Pedestrians walk only on marked, barricaded routes. At a crossing: stop, make eye contact with the operator, wait for the wave.
  • Hi-vis on outside any cab or shelter: Class 2 by day, Class 3 at night, or a hi-vis cooling vest.
  • Haze: lights and beacon on whenever a blowing-dust advisory is posted. Can't see 300 ft? 5 mph. Can't see the road edge or the vehicle ahead? Pull off at a pull-out or the road edge, hazards on, stay in the cab unless told to move to shelter, call Ch 12 (site rule).
  • Reversing: plan routes so you drive through. Backing where you can't see? Use a spotter. Backup alarm and camera working. Nobody walks behind a machine.
  • Crew shuttles: seat belts on. No riders in truck beds, on running boards or on equipment. Drivers carry the badge endorsement.
  • Refueling or recharging: at marked fuel or charge points only. Engine off. Bond the hose (dry air builds static). Extinguisher in reach. No hot work within 35 ft.

Forklifts & telehandlers

  • Only operators with the badge endorsement for that machine, trained and evaluated on it.
  • Read the load chart for the boom angle, extension and attachment. Over the chart = no lift.
  • No riders. Never lift people on forks or in a bucket.
  • Travel with the load low and tilted back. Seat belt on. Look where you're going.
  • Firm, level ground. Stabilizers down on mats where the chart needs them. Loose sand and side slopes tip machines.
  • Parked: forks or boom down, brake set, shut off.
  • Hook or winch lifting a suspended load? It's a crane lift. All crane rules apply, including a qualified rigger and signal person.

Structure printers

  • Printers are heavy equipment on Ch 12, call sign "Printer [Sector] [n]" (Printer Golf 2).
  • The gantry and head move without warning on their program. Stay outside the printer barrier while it runs.
  • Barrier gates are interlocked: open one and the printer stops. Never bypass or tape an interlock (site rule). Found one bypassed? Stop work and tell your foreman.
  • Inside the barrier only with the printer locked out, your own lock on (HS-07).
  • Fresh FerroLattice-7 bead comes out at about 500°F. Burns and fumes: HS-08.
  • Gantry rails are trip and crush points. Never stand between the gantry and the structure.

Manual handling

  • Over 50 lb, or too long to control alone? Two people or a lifting aid (site rule).
  • Post-stasis muscles are weaker. Ramp days, light duty or a failed grip check: 25 lb one-person limit (site rule). Anything heavier is a team lift or a lifting aid.
  • Panels are sails. At 15+ mph: two people on any panel bigger than 4 × 4 ft, or move it by machine. At 20+ mph: no hand-carrying panels in the open (site rule).
  • Two-person panel carries count as very heavy work on the DCW work/rest table (HS-03). Heat plus heavy lifting raises the risk of rhabdo.
  • Keep the load close. Turn your feet; don't twist. Kneel pads for floor work. Rotate repetitive jobs like rebar tying.

Night lighting

  • Landing zones, rigging areas, haul-road crossings and trench edges are lit before night work starts.
  • Signal persons use lighted wands or gloves at night.
  • Lights fail during a lift: the operator stops and holds the load. It's landed only on the signal person's direction once light is back.
  • Operator can't clearly see the load, the landing zone or the signal? The lift stops.
  • Glare in the operator's eyes? Re-aim the light before the lift.
  • Light levels: Supplement D (HS-SD).

Trenches & excavations

Sand caves in without warning

Our soil is Type C

Dry, loose sand is the least stable soil class. A cubic yard of it weighs about as much as a car. Walls collapse with no cracks or warning.

5 ftProtective system always
34°Max slope: 1½ ft back for 1 ft down
2 ftSpoils back from edge

Protective system means sloping the walls back 1½ ft for every 1 ft of depth (34° from flat), starting at the trench floor, shoring, or a trench shield (box). No vertical walls in our soil. Deeper than 20 ft needs an engineer's design.

Under 5 ft is not automatically safe. The competent person checks it. In our loose sand, if the walls can move at all, it gets sloped or shored, however shallow. A kneeling worker can be buried in a 4 ft trench.

Trench rules

  • The competent person (excavation) inspects every excavation before each shift, after every sandstorm, flood or rain, and whenever conditions change. They pull everyone out at any sign of movement.
  • Ladder, ramp or stair within 25 ft of every worker in trenches 4 ft deep or more.
  • Spoil piles, tools and equipment at least 2 ft back from the edge.
  • Never work outside the shield or in an unprotected section, even "just for a minute."
  • Trench box: dig no more than 2 ft below its bottom. Nobody stays in it while it's moved.
  • Equipment near the edge: barricades, stop logs or a spotter. Vibration and weight start collapses.
  • Nobody under a bucket or a load being dug or lifted.
  • Crossings over trenches 6 ft or deeper have guardrails. Barricade unattended trenches.
  • Flood warning or flash-storm water: everyone out at once. Back in only when the competent person clears it.

Dig permit & buried services

  • No dig without a PTW-DG dig permit at the job. Survey and Electrical & Power locate and mark buried services; the area foreman signs.
  • Within 2 ft of a mark: hand dig or vacuum dig (site rule).
  • Sand buries and blows away paint. Marks also get flags or stakes. Re-check them after a sandstorm.
  • Hit a line? Stop, back away, keep people clear, tell your foreman. Power or gas: Ch 1 "Electrical" or "Gas".
  • Red: power
  • Orange: data, comms
  • Yellow: gas, fuel, steam
  • Blue: drinking water
  • Purple: recycled water
  • Green: sewer, drains
  • Pink: survey marks
  • White: planned dig

Air in trenches

  • Open trenches follow HS-06, using the air limits in Supplement A (HS-SA).
  • Shafts, tunnels, and any trench with a hazardous atmosphere or an engulfment risk outside the shield are permit-required spaces under HS-SA, with a PTW-CS signed by the entry supervisor.
  • Test the air before anyone enters a trench over 4 ft deep where gas can collect, and keep testing while people work there. Exhaust, fuel vapor and purge gas sink into low cuts.
  • Limits: O₂ 19.5–23.5%. Flammable gas under 10% LEL. Toxic gas limits on HS-SA. Outside the limits? Everyone out.

Contact or collapse

A crane or machine touches a power line, a load drops, or a trench caves in.

ALL STOP first.
Then call it in.

Would-be rescuers often become the next victims. A second collapse often follows the first.

  • Call "ALL STOP, ALL STOP" on your channel, then Ch 1: "Emergency, emergency, emergency" + type (Electrical, Struck-by, Caught-in, Structural) + location + number hurt + hazards + your call sign. Location like "Echo-7, trench, north end." Stay on Ch 1 until Hub Command or the medic releases you.
  • Power line contact: operator stays in the cab unless it's on fire. If it's safe, try to move the boom away from the line. Assume it's still live even if the sparks stop: lines can re-energize by themselves. If you must get out, jump clear with feet together, never touching machine and ground at once, then shuffle away in small steps. Everyone else stays back at least 35 ft. Nobody touches the machine, load or taglines until Electrical & Power says it's dead.
  • Trench collapse: don't jump in to dig. Keep everyone out, shut down nearby equipment, and wait for Fire & Rescue with shoring.
  • Dropped load: don't lift it off a person with a crane unless Fire & Rescue directs it. Keep the area clear.

Stop-work authority

Anyone can stop unsafe work. No penalty, no questions. Stop it, make it safe, tell your crew lead (your foreman if there's no crew lead; any green hat if it isn't fixed). Work restarts only when the crew lead confirms the fix with the person who stopped it. Every stop is logged. That includes any lift or machine that puts someone in danger.

Report it

Report every injury, illness, first aid case, near miss, damage and unsafe condition to your crew lead before the end of the shift (immediately for anything serious), on form HS-IR with your Colonist ID. Don't move anything except to rescue. Safety & Health investigates with the crew; findings are shared at muster. No retaliation. Every dropped load, line strike, power-line contact and trench movement counts.

Worker acknowledgement

I have read this poster. I will never go under a suspended load. I know who signals my lifts and on which talkgroup, I know what happens when the radio drops, and I know I can stop any lift or machine that puts someone in danger.
