Read the label.

The polymers, resins and coatings that build this city can blind you, burn you or ruin your lungs for life.

Sensitized?

Some chemicals only give you one warning.

Isocyanates (spray foams, polyurethane coatings) and epoxy resins can make your body allergic to them. It can happen after one big exposure or after months of small ones. Once you are sensitized, even a trace can set off an asthma attack or a skin reaction. It does not go away.

Sensitized workers are moved off that material for good. Report wheezing, chest tightness, or an itchy rash on your hands or face to the Forward Aid Station the same shift.

The 9 hazard symbols

GHS pictograms · on every container

Red diamond, black symbol, white background. If a container has no label, don't use it. Tag it and call Ch 15 Quartermaster.

Danger

The more severe hazards. Can kill, blind, cause cancer, or burn on contact.

Warning

Less severe, still harmful. Irritation, mild burns, harm with repeated exposure.

Every label carries one signal word, never both. The label also lists the hazard statements ("Causes severe skin burns") and what to do ("Wear protective gloves").

The fire diamond

  • Blue · Health
  • Red · Flammability
  • Yellow · Instability (reactivity)
  • White · Special: W = reacts with water, OX = oxidizer, SA = simple asphyxiant
  • 0 = minimal, 4 = severe. Example: QuickSet C-4X hardener (H3, F1, I1).

Fire & Rescue reads this diamond from 50 ft away. Keep it visible on every store and tank.

Site materials

Hazard cards · full SDS at the chem store
Printable structural polymer

FerroLattice-7

Health hazardExclamation mark

Extruded at about 500°F by the structure printers. Printing releases ultrafine particles and irritating fumes. The bead stays hot enough to burn for minutes after it looks set.

PPE: heat gloves, face shield at the head, half-mask with P100 + organic vapor cartridges inside print enclosures. Printer exhaust on before the head heats.

Two-part fast-cure composite resin

QuickSet C-4X

CorrosionHealth hazardExclamation mark

Epoxy-isocyanate hybrid. Causes allergic skin rash and asthma. Mixing makes heat. A large mixed batch can run away, smoke, and ignite. Desert heat shortens pot life.

PPE: nitrile gloves (double), goggles, sleeves, respirator when spraying. Mix only the pot size on the label. Put leftover mix in the sand-filled cure bucket, spread thin, outside.

Nano-ceramic heat-shield coating

VeilCoat

FlameHealth hazardExclamation mark

Sprayed on hulls and roofs to reflect sun. The solvent carrier is flammable. Spray mist carries nanoparticles deep into the lungs.

PPE: full-face respirator with P100 + organic vapor cartridges, coveralls, gloves. No hot work within 35 ft while spraying or until the solvent flashes off (see HS-05).

Sand-polymer binder (local sand)

Dunecrete

CorrosionHealth hazard

Strongly alkaline when wet, like cement. Causes chemical burns that start painless and get worse for hours. Dry mix and cutting cured Dunecrete make silica dust (see HS-02).

PPE: alkali-resistant gloves and boots, goggles, long sleeves. Wet cutting or vacuum shrouds only. Wash skin contact with water right away.

Old Earth hazards that still apply

Real chemistry behind the site materials

Isocyanates

In spray foams and polyurethane coatings. The leading cause of work-related asthma. Spray only with supplied air or the respirator on the permit. Keep bystanders out of the spray zone.

Epoxy resins

Uncured resin and hardener cause allergic dermatitis. It starts as a red, itchy rash on hands and wrists. Never wipe resin off skin with solvent. Use soap and water.

Curing heat

Resins give off heat as they cure. Mass speeds it up. A bucket of mixed resin can reach smoking temperature. Never leave mixed batches in a closed container or a trash bin.

Styrene

Vapor from polyester and vinyl ester resins. Headache, dizziness, eye and throat irritation. Ventilate, and use organic vapor cartridges in tight spaces.

Fiber dust

Cutting or sanding carbon fiber and fiberglass throws fine fibers and resin dust. They irritate skin, eyes and lungs. Wet cut or vacuum shroud, P100 respirator, sealed goggles.

Burning polymers

Polyurethane smoke releases hydrogen cyanide. PVC releases hydrogen chloride. All of them make carbon monoxide. Never fight a polymer fire without breathing air. Get out and call it in.

Splash

10 seconds to water.
15 minutes under it.

Every eyewash and drench shower on site is within 10 seconds of the hazard, on the same level, with a clear path (ANSI Z358.1). Know where yours is before you open a container.

  1. Eyes: get to the eyewash. Hold your eyelids open and roll your eyes. Flush at least 15 minutes. Take out contact lenses while you flush.
  2. Skin or clothing: under the drench shower. Pull off contaminated clothing while the water runs. Flush 15 minutes.
  3. Breathed it in: get to fresh air. Don't go back in for anyone without breathing air.
  4. Ch 1: "Emergency, emergency, emergency," with sector-grid, deck, the material, and how many are hurt. Then Ch 14 for the medic.
  5. Bring the SDS or the container label to the medic. Section 4 tells them what to do.

Every shift

  • Check the label and the SDS before you open anything new to you.
  • Wear the PPE in SDS section 8. Not the PPE you happen to have.
  • Cap it when you're not pouring. Open containers off-gas in the heat.
  • Never decant into an unlabeled container. Secondary containers get a label too.
  • Wash hands before eating, drinking or using the latrine. Resin on your hands ends up on your face.
  • Spills: stop the source if it's safe, keep people back, call Ch 15 for the spill kit. Big or unknown spill: Ch 1.

Unknown? Don't touch.

No label, a leaking drum, a smell you don't recognize, or a material you haven't been trained on: stop work, keep people back and call your foreman. No penalty, no questions.
