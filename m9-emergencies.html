When it goes wrong.

15,000 people on one site means emergencies will happen. What you do in the first five minutes decides who lives.

Know the sirens

Site-wide · Every sector · Every shelter
Take shelterContinuous long tone. Sandstorm or atmospheric hazard. Go to the nearest sealed shelter now.
EvacuateAlternating high-low. Stop work, make your equipment safe, walk to your muster point.
All stopThree short blasts. Every crane, machine and torch stops moving. Hold in place and listen to Ch 1.
All clearOne long tone after any of the above. Wait for your crew lead before you go back to work.

The emergency call

Channel 1. Speak slowly. Plain language. No codes.

"Emergency, emergency, emergency.
[type] Fall.
[location] Charlie-14, Deck 3.
[hurt] Two hurt, one not moving.
[hazards] Loose steel overhead.
[you] This is Charlie Crew 14-3."

Then stay on Ch 1 and answer questions. Everyone else stays off Ch 1 until it clears. Types: Heat casualty, Man down, Fall, Fire, Gas, Structural, Struck-by, Caught-in, Electrical, Confined space, Sandstorm.

You're first on scene? You're in command.

  • One commander at a time. The first person on scene runs it until someone more qualified takes over.
  • Make it safe for you first. Stop equipment, kill power, look up. A second casualty helps nobody.
  • Give jobs by name. "You, call it in. You, get the trauma kit. You, keep people back."
  • Hand off out loud. "I'm handing command to the Charlie Super. Here's what we have." The new commander says "I have command."
  • Write down times: when it happened, when the tourniquet went on, when the medic arrived.

Stop the bleed

Someone can bleed to death in under five minutes. You are faster than any medic.

Pressure. Pack.
Tourniquet.

Every crew carries a trauma kit with two tourniquets. Know where yours is.

  1. Find the bleeding. Cut or open clothing so you can see the wound.
  2. Push hard with both hands, straight down on the wound. Keep pushing.
  3. Deep wound in the neck, armpit or groin? Pack it full of gauze or clean cloth, then push hard on top.
  4. Arm or leg bleeding that won't stop? Tourniquet 2–3 inches above the wound, never on a joint. Tighten until the bleeding stops. It will hurt. That's right.
  5. Write the time on the tourniquet or their forehead. Never loosen it. Medics take it off.

CPR

  1. Not responding and not breathing normally? Call it on Ch 1 and send someone for the AED.
  2. Heel of your hand in the center of the chest. Push hard and fast. Let the chest come all the way back up.
  3. Turn on the AED and do what it says. Keep going until a medic takes over.
  4. Swap pushers every 2 minutes. Tired hands push too shallow.

Fire

  • Pull the pin.
  • Aim low, at the base of the fire.
  • Squeeze the handle.
  • Sweep side to side.

Leave instead if the fire is bigger than a waste bin, is spreading, is filling the space with smoke, or is between you and the exit. Keep your back to the way out. Call it on Ch 1 and Ch 13. Fire & Rescue wear red helmets: do what they say.

Sandstorm

  1. Continuous siren or Ch 16 alert: stop, make equipment safe, and walk to the nearest sealed shelter. Don't run for your own sector.
  2. Wrap your face, goggles on. Keep a hand on your buddy or a rope line. Visibility can drop to nothing.
  3. Seal the shelter. Close both doors of the airlock and start the filters.
  4. Headcount by crew. The senior person reports the shelter count to the sector channel.
  5. Stay until the all-clear. Don't open the door to look.

Flash flood

Rare rain upstream fills dry channels in minutes, even under a clear sky over you.

  • Get out of wadis, trenches and low cuts the moment Ch 16 calls rain.
  • Climb. Higher ground beats faster ground.
  • Never walk or drive into moving water.

Evacuate & muster

  1. Alternating siren: stop, shut down, close valves and torches, lower loads if it's safe.
  2. Walk to your crew's muster point, like MP-C1. Use the assigned route. Never go back for tools.
  3. Crew lead counts heads and reports on the sector channel:
"Charlie Super, Charlie Crew 14-3.
At MP-C1. Twelve assigned, eleven present.
Missing: one, last seen Deck 3, east stair.
No injuries. Over."

Missing people are searched for by Rescue, not by you. Report where they were last seen.

Mass casualty: stay on your crew

  • Don't self-dispatch. A thousand helpers arriving at once block the medics, the cranes and the road.
  • Stay with your crew and your crew lead. Your lead will be told where you're needed.
  • Crews get roles: litter carriers, scene security, runners, triage helpers, traffic control for ambulances.
  • Keep Ch 1 and Ch 14 clear. Only the people working the incident talk.
  • Walking wounded go to the Forward Aid Station. Central Med at the Hub takes reds first.
  • After it's over, everyone involved gets a medical and a debrief. It's normal to be shaken.

Look before you leap

Make the scene safe before you go in. Stop the machine, kill the power, check the air, look up for loads. Rescuers who rush in become the next casualties.

Worker acknowledgement

I know the four siren signals, my muster point, my nearest shelter, where my crew's trauma kit and AED are, and how to make an emergency call on Ch 1.
