Hand signals.

In full view of the operator, your arms do the talking. Lose sight, and the lift stops.

Read first

One signal person. Anyone can stop.

Each lift has one qualified signal person (orange hard hat, badge endorsement). The operator takes signals only from them, except STOP and EMERGENCY STOP, which the operator obeys from anyone. Signals are given facing the operator, in clear view. If the operator loses sight of the signal person or the load, the operator stops.

Radio drops during a lift? The operator stops all motion and holds the load. Resume only when the radio is back and tested, or when the signal person is in the operator's full, continuous view and the operator, signal person and whoever directs the lift (the Lift Director, or the lift lead on a routine lift) agree to switch to hand signals. Blind lifts stay stopped. Radio signals go on the lift talkgroup named on the lift plan, never on Ch 11 (REF-RAD, HS-06).

Site signals are for people, never for operators, except ALL STOP (the EMERGENCY STOP signal). Give EMERGENCY STOP first, then the site signal once the load is held.

At night, use lighted wands or lighted gloves. Sand, haze or dark so thick the operator can't clearly see every signal? Stop the lift.

Figures face you, the way the operator sees the signal person. Orange arrows show the motion.

Crane signals

Old Earth standard · ASME B30.5 mobile cranes

Crawler and telescoping-boom signals apply only to those cranes. Any signal not on this sheet is used only if the operator, signal person and whoever directs the lift (the Lift Director, or the lift lead on a routine lift) agree on it before the lift.

Equipment spotter signals

Backing and positioning haul trucks, loaders, carriers

Spotters stand where the operator can see them in a mirror or camera, never directly behind the machine. Lose sight of your spotter: stop. Machine horns are about that machine only, never site signals: 1 = stopping, 2 = moving forward, 3 = backing up. Hear 3 horn blasts near you? Get out of its path.

DCW site emergency signals

Site rule · Dunewright only, not an Old Earth standard

Site signals are for people, never for operators, except ALL STOP (EMERGENCY STOP). Near a lift, give EMERGENCY STOP first, then the site signal once the load is held. After any emergency signal, call it in on Ch 1 as soon as you can: "Emergency, emergency, emergency," type, location, number hurt, hazards, your call sign. The caller stays on Ch 1 until Hub Command or the medic releases them.

Fighting a fire (HS-09): fight it only if it's waste-bin size, your exit is behind you, and you have the right extinguisher. Not out in about 10 seconds? Leave and call Ch 1 "Fire". Never fight a polymer, power-cell or gas-fed fire. A polymer fire is a fire in bulk feedstock, a curing resin mass, a printed structure or panel stack, or a spray zone. A waste-bin-size fire in rags or scrap may be fought. Smother a rag can with its lid. A fire that's out: tell your foreman, who logs it on Ch 13.

STOP beats everything

One arm out, palm down, swung forward and back = STOP. Both arms = EMERGENCY STOP, the site ALL STOP. Every operator obeys it from anyone: cranes, machines and hot work stop, and everyone holds position. A suspended load is landed only on the signal person's direction. The voice version is "ALL STOP, ALL STOP" on any channel. The siren version is 3 short blasts. Cleared only by whoever called it, or by command.
