Hand signals.

When the radio is drowned out by engines, wind or sand, your arms do the talking.

Read first

One signal person. Anyone can stop.

Each lift has one qualified signal person in an orange hard hat. The operator takes signals only from them, except STOP and EMERGENCY STOP, which the operator obeys from anyone. Signals are given facing the operator, in clear view. If the operator loses sight of the signal person, they stop. Lost the radio? Switch to hand signals and confirm on Ch 11 when it's back.

Figures face you, the way the operator sees the signal person. Orange arrows show the motion.

Crane signals

Old Earth standard · ASME B30.5 mobile cranes

Equipment spotter signals

Backing and positioning haul trucks, loaders, carriers

Spotters stand where the operator can see them in a mirror or camera, never directly behind the machine. Lose sight of your spotter: stop.

DCW site emergency signals

Site signals · Dunewright only, not an Old Earth standard

After any emergency signal, call it in on Ch 1 as soon as you can: "Emergency, emergency, emergency", type, sector-grid, deck, number hurt.

STOP beats everything

Two arms out, palms down, waving back and forth means everything stops now: cranes, machines, hot work. It works the same on the crane line, the haul road and the deck. The siren version is 3 short blasts.
