How to make a call

  1. Listen first. Don't talk over someone.
  2. Press, wait one second, then speak. The first word gets clipped otherwise.
  3. Who you want, then who you are. Say it once.
  4. Say the message. Keep it under 20 seconds.
  5. End with "over" if you need a reply, "out" if you're done.
"Charlie Super, this is Charlie Crew 14-3, over."
"Charlie Crew 14-3, Charlie Super, go ahead."
"Deck 3 steel is set. Requesting inspection, over."
"Copy. Safety Charlie inbound one-five minutes. Out."

Never say "over and out". "Over" asks for a reply and "out" ends the call.

Emergency call

Channel 1. Plain language only. No codes, no slang.

Type. Where. How many.
Hazards. Who.

When you hear "Emergency" on Ch 1, stop talking on every channel until the medic or command clears it.

"Emergency, emergency, emergency.
Struck-by.
Delta-22, Deck 0, haul road east.
One hurt, conscious, leg bleeding.
Hauler stopped, engine off.
This is Delta Crew 22-1."
"ALL STOP, ALL STOP." Any channel. Anyone. Every crane, machine and torch in earshot stops moving until whoever called it, or command, clears it.

Phonetic alphabet

Spell grids, part numbers and names

Say numbers digit by digit: "one-four" for 14. Say "niner" for 9 so it isn't heard as "nein" or "five". Some crews also use "tree" for 3 and "fife" for 5.

Check-ins

  • Every crew lead checks in with their foreman every 30 minutes on the sector channel.
  • Format: "Charlie Foreman 14, Charlie Crew 14-3, check-in. Twelve on deck, all good, out."
  • A crew that misses a check-in gets one call back. Miss two and someone walks over to check in person.
  • Confined space entrants talk to their attendant continuously, not every 30 minutes.
  • Crews scan Ch 16 for heat level changes and storm alerts.

Lost comms

  1. Try another channel, then try again from higher ground.
  2. Still nothing? Stop work that needs a radio. No crane lifts and no blind equipment moves.
  3. Send a runner to your foreman, or the whole crew walks to your muster point or last check-in point.
  4. Use the site hand signals until radios are back.
  5. If a siren sounds while you're out of contact, follow the siren.

Radio discipline

Think, then key. No chatter, no music, no jokes on work channels. Never sit on the transmit key. Use call signs, not names. Keep Ch 1 clear unless someone's life or the site is in danger.
