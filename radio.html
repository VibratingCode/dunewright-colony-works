Talk short. Talk clear.

Over 15,000 people share these channels. A sloppy call can bury the one that saves a life.

Scan rule

  • Every radio scans Ch 1 + Ch 16 + your sector channel. Crane, equipment and hot-work crews add their trade channel (11, 12 or 13).
  • Ch 1 is set as the priority channel. It cuts in over everything else (site rule).
  • Hub Command rebroadcasts every siren and every sandstorm, flood and lightning warning on Ch 1.
  • Check your scan list at every radio check.

Talkgroups

  • Foreman talkgroup, e.g. "C-F14" (Charlie, Foreman 14). Crew check-ins and routine crew traffic. "Crew channel" means your foreman talkgroup.
  • Lift talkgroup, e.g. "Lift C-07". Every lift gets its own, named on the lift plan. Signals that move loads go only there. Ch 11 is coordination only, never for moving loads.
  • During a lift the operator's radio is hands-free, tested before the lift, and does not scan. The lift plan names someone else to scan Ch 1 + Ch 16 and relay warnings on the lift talkgroup. Sirens and strobes still reach the cab.
  • Entry talkgroup: a confined-space entry uses the talkgroup named on its PTW-CS (Supplement A (HS-SA)).

Emergency call

Channel 1. Plain language only. No codes, no slang.

Type. Where. How many.
Hazards. Who.

Types: Heat casualty · Man down · Fall · Fire · Gas · Chemical · Structural · Struck-by · Caught-in · Electrical · Confined space · Flood · Sandstorm. A short qualifier after the type is fine ("Fall, worker suspended"). A burn with nothing still burning is "Man down".

"Emergency, emergency, emergency.
[type] Struck-by.
[location] Delta-22, Deck 0, haul road east.
[hurt] One hurt, conscious, leg bleeding.
[hazards] Hauler stopped, engine off.
[you] This is Delta Crew 22-1."
  • The caller stays on Ch 1 until Hub Command or the medic releases them.
  • Everyone else stays off Ch 1 (and off Ch 14 during a mass casualty).
  • Other channels keep working. Land the load, park the machine, keep talking to your entrant.
  • Workers use Ch 14 only when told.

All stop

"ALL STOP, ALL STOP."
  • Anyone can call it, on any channel. Three short siren blasts mean the same.
  • Stop all motion, hot work and machine operation. Hold position.
  • A suspended load is landed only on the signal person's direction.
  • Cleared on the same channel by whoever called it, or by command.

Lost comms

During a lift: the operator stops all motion and holds the load. Resume only when the radio is back and tested, or when the signal person is in the operator's full, continuous view and the operator, signal person and whoever directs the lift (the Lift Director, or the lift lead on a routine lift) agree to switch to hand signals (REF-HS). Blind lifts stay stopped.

Anywhere else:

  1. Try another channel, then try again from higher ground.
  2. Still nothing? Stop work that needs a radio. No blind equipment moves.
  3. Send a buddy pair to your foreman. Nobody goes alone at Caution heat or higher.
  4. Missed your check-in? If the call-back gets no answer within 5 minutes, your foreman sends someone in person. Stay where your foreman expects you.
  5. Siren while you're out of contact? Take cover or evacuate on the siren. Leave cover only on the voice all clear, passed on by your crew lead or a runner.

What to do in each emergency: HS-09.

Check-ins

  • Buddies watch each other continuously.
  • The crew lead sees every crew member at least every 30 minutes.
  • The crew lead checks in with the foreman every 30 minutes on the foreman talkgroup.
"Charlie Foreman 14, Charlie Crew 14-3, check-in.
Twelve on deck, all good. Out."
  • Missed check-in: immediate call-back. No answer within 5 minutes: someone goes in person.
  • Lone work (only below Caution, never at height, in confined spaces or near wadis or trenches): 15-minute check-ins and a logged location.
  • Confined space: the attendant checks in with each entrant at least every 5 minutes, by the method named on the PTW-CS.

Headcounts

Muster: crew leads report in person to the muster warden (a named crew lead at each muster point). The warden reports once to the Super on the sector channel.

"Charlie Super, Muster MP-C1.
Fourteen crews. One hundred sixty-two assigned, one hundred sixty present. Two missing, details follow. Over."

Shelter: the senior person reports as "Shelter [Sector]-[grid]" on that shelter's sector channel. Missing persons go on Ch 1.

Crew leads also tell their own department where their people are.

Always say the label word (Crew, Foreman, Hauler, Shelter, Muster) so a call sign never sounds like a location. Lost badge? Tell your crew lead the same shift. The crew lead reports it to the Security Desk. Roles and stations: REF-ORG · REF-GL.

How to make a call

  1. Listen first. Don't talk over someone.
  2. Press, wait one second, then speak. The first word gets clipped otherwise.
  3. Who you want, then who you are. Say it once.
  4. Say the message. Keep it under 20 seconds.
  5. End with "over" if you need a reply, "out" if you're done.
On talkgroup C-F14:
"Charlie Foreman 14, this is Charlie Crew 14-3, over."
"Charlie Crew 14-3, Charlie Foreman 14, go ahead."
"Charlie-14, Tower B, Deck 3 steel is set. Requesting inspection, over."
"Copy. Safety Charlie 3 inbound, one-five minutes. Out."

Radio checkHow do you hear me? Answer "loud and clear" or "weak".

Never say "over and out". "Over" asks for a reply and "out" ends the call.

Phonetic alphabet

Spell grids, part numbers and names

Numbers

  • Digit by digit for grids, IDs, channels and call signs: 14 is "one-four". Charlie Crew 14-3 is "Charlie Crew one-four dash three".
  • 9 is "niner", so it isn't heard as "five" or "nein".
  • Headcounts may be spoken as words: "twelve on deck".
  • No other alternate pronunciations.

Location format

Sector-grid, structure, deck, landmark. Grid numbers are painted on every grid post.

  • "Charlie-14, Tower B, Deck 3, east stair."
  • Open ground: "Delta-22, Deck 0, haul road east."
  • Trenches: "Echo-7, trench, north end."
  • Below grade: "Deck minus 1".

Radio discipline

Think, then key. No chatter, no music, no jokes on work channels. Never sit on the transmit key. Use call signs. Never use names on air. Keep Ch 1 clear unless someone's life or the site is in danger. Check your radio and scan list at the start of every shift.
