Badge endorsements

No badge entry, no task

The rules

  • No badge entry, no task. Crew leads and foremen check the badge before they assign work.
  • Pre-stasis qualifications are re-verified by a practical test before use. Stasis dulls skill and reaction time. An old card counts for nothing until you pass here.
  • Module or supplement first, then the endorsement, then the task.
  • An expired endorsement is suspended until you refresh it. The badge shows each expiry date. (site rule)
  • Retrain early after an incident or near miss in that task, after unsafe work is seen, or for new equipment or methods. (site rule)
  • Records are kept by the Director's staff.

Your badge lists

  • Completed modules and supplements.
  • Endorsements for machines, permits and roles, with expiry dates.
  • Respirator fit-test date.
  • Heat-ramp status.

Lost badge: tell your crew lead the same shift. The crew lead reports it to the Security Desk. Never lend your badge.

Glossary

Every term on every page · A to Z
Radio prowords: REF-RAD

Acronyms & units

AED
Automated external defibrillator. Talks you through shocking a stopped heart. At every FAS, shelter, structure cooling station and lift muster.
ALL STOP
"ALL STOP, ALL STOP" on any channel, or 3 short siren blasts. Stop all motion, hot work and machine operation, and hold position.
ANSI · ASME
Old Earth standards bodies: American National Standards Institute; American Society of Mechanical Engineers (crane and sling standards B30).
APF
Assigned protection factor. How much cleaner a respirator keeps your air when it fits and is worn right. APF 10 means one tenth of the outside level.
AR · FR
Arc-rated and flame-resistant clothing. FR resists catching fire. AR is also tested and rated against arc flash.
Ch
Radio channel. Ch 1 is Site Emergency. Full plan on REF-RAD.
CO
Carbon monoxide. Toxic gas with no smell or color, from engines, fires and burning polymers. Entry limit under 35 ppm.
CO₂
Carbon dioxide. Your body senses rising CO₂. It can't sense missing oxygen, so low oxygen gives no warning.
CPR
Cardiopulmonary resuscitation. Chest pushes 100–120 a minute, 2–2.4 in deep.
dBA
Decibels, A-weighted: sound level as your ear hears it. Every 3 dB more is double the sound energy. Hearing protection at 85 dBA (site rule).
DCW
Dunewright Colony Works, the employer.
FAS
Forward Aid Station. One per sector, call sign "[Sector] Aid". All injuries and illness go here.
fc
Foot-candle: the light falling on a surface. About 10.8 lux. Night minimums are on HS-SD.
GF
General foreman.
GFCI
Ground-fault circuit interrupter. Cuts the power when as little as 4–6 milliamps leaks to ground, for example through you.
GHS
Globally Harmonized System for chemical labels: red-diamond pictograms, one signal word (Danger or Warning), hazard statements.
GMAW · FCAW · GTAW · SMAW
Welding processes: MIG, flux core, TIG and stick.
H₂S
Hydrogen sulfide. Toxic gas that smells of rotten eggs, then kills your sense of smell. Entry limit under 10 ppm.
HEPA
High-efficiency particulate air filter, on dust collectors and vacuums.
HS-IR
Incident report form. Filed with your Colonist ID.
ICS
Incident Command System. One commander at a time, jobs given by name, command handed over out loud.
IH
Industrial Hygiene. Samples air, silica, noise and chemicals; runs the respirator and hearing programs. Call sign "Hygiene [n]".
kV
Kilovolt: 1,000 volts.
LEL
Lower explosive limit: the least gas or vapor in air that can ignite. Meters read % of LEL. Entry stops at 10%. No hot work at 1% or above (site rule).
LP-
Critical lift plan number.
MEWP
Mobile elevating work platform: boom lifts and scissor lifts. Supplement C (HS-SC).
MP
Muster point, MP-A1 to MP-H2. Two per sector.
N95
Disposable filtering facepiece. Filters at least 95% of particles. No use against oil mist, gas or low oxygen. APF 10.
NFPA 704
The fire diamond. Blue health, red flammability, yellow instability, white special hazards. 0 is minimal, 4 is severe.
NIOSH
Old Earth US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health. Sets RELs.
NWS
Old Earth US National Weather Service. Source of the heat index.
O₂
Oxygen. Safe range 19.5–23.5%.
OSHA
Old Earth US Occupational Safety and Health Administration. Sets PELs and most standards on these posters.
OV
Organic vapor: the respirator cartridge for solvent vapors.
P100
Particulate filter that stops 99.97% of particles, oil mist included. Fits half- and full-face respirators.
PAPR
Powered air-purifying respirator. A battery blower pushes filtered air into a hood or mask.
PEL
Permissible exposure limit: OSHA's legal limit, usually an 8-hour TWA.
PPE
Personal protective equipment.
ppm
Parts per million.
psig
Pounds per square inch, gauge.
PTW
Permit to work: PTW-HW hot work, PTW-CS confined space, PTW-HE heat entry, PTW-EE energized electrical, PTW-DG dig permit, PTW-SP spray permit. All on REF-PTW.
QA/QC
Quality assurance and quality control. Grey hats.
REL
Recommended exposure limit: NIOSH's health-based limit, often stricter than the PEL. Usually a TWA over a workday of up to 10 hours.
ROPS
Rollover protective structure: the cage on a machine. It protects you only with your seat belt on.
SDS
Safety data sheet. 16 sections. At the chem store and on the site terminal.
SIMOPS
Simultaneous operations: jobs close enough to endanger each other. Checked before a permit is signed (REF-PTW).
SRL
Self-retracting lifeline. A lifeline on a reel that locks in a fall, like a seat belt.
SRU
Stasis Revival Unit. Runs Revival Med Bay. Red hat with a white cross. Call sign "Revival [n]".
START
Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment. Sorts casualties in about 30 seconds each: green, yellow, red, black.
TB MED 507
Old Earth US Army heat bulletin. Basis of the DCW work/rest table.
TWA
Time-weighted average: your average exposure over a shift.
UV
Ultraviolet light, from the sun and welding arcs. Burns eyes and skin, and weakens slings and helmets.
WBGT
Wet-bulb globe temperature. A heat-stress reading that counts air temperature, humidity, sun and wind. Posted by your crew lead at muster and hourly.
Z87
Mark on safety eyewear that meets ANSI Z87.1.
µg/m³
Micrograms per cubic meter. Silica PEL 50, action level 25.

Site & safety terms

Acclimatizing
Days 6–14 of the heat ramp. Full work/rest table. Your crew lead checks you every 15 min at WBGT 85+.
Action level
The exposure where monitoring and medical checks start. Silica: 25 µg/m³.
All clear
One 15-second tone, then Hub Command on Ch 1: "All clear, all clear, [area]." Never act on the tone alone.
Anchor
The fixed point your lanyard or SRL clips to. 5,000 lb per worker, or designed by a qualified person with a 2:1 safety factor.
Approach boundary
Shock boundary around exposed live parts (NFPA 70E). Unqualified workers stop at the limited approach boundary and cross only with a qualified worker who briefs them and stays with them. Supplement B (HS-SB).
Arc eye
A UV burn on the surface of the eye from a welding arc. The pain starts hours later.
Arc flash
An explosive burst of heat and light from an electrical fault. Supplement B (HS-SB).
Attendant
Stays outside a confined space for the whole entry, counts entrants, orders them out and calls rescue. Never enters.
Authorized employee
A worker trained to lock out a machine with their own red lock.
Badge endorsement
A badge entry that clears you for a machine, permit or role. No badge entry, no task.
Blind lift
A lift where the operator can't see the load or the signal person. Radio only. Stays stopped if comms drop.
Blowing-dust advisory
Visibility 1 mile or less. No outdoor hot work.
Buddy
Your assigned partner. You watch each other all shift.
Call sign
The radio name of a station. It stays with the station when people rotate (REF-RAD).
Capacitor bank
Holds charge after the power is off. Discharge it and keep it grounded.
Central Med
The hospital at the Hub: serious cases (reds), surgery, ICU.
Check-in
Crew lead to foreman every 30 min on the foreman talkgroup. Missed: immediate call-back, then someone goes in person after 5 min.
Class E
Hard hat tested at 20,000 volts (ANSI Z89.1). Worn by electricians.
Class 2 · Class 3
Hi-vis garment classes (ANSI/ISEA 107). Class 3 has sleeves and the most reflective area. Class 2 by day, Class 3 at night.
Colonist ID
Your ID number. On every sign-off, every HS-IR and your personal lock.
Competent person
A trained worker named by the foreman for excavation, scaffolds or fall protection, able to spot hazards and with authority to stop work. Old Earth meaning (OSHA 1926.32): can identify existing and predictable hazards and is authorized to fix them promptly.
Cooling station
Shade, water and an ice trough on every deck of an active structure. Also the shift weigh station.
Crew channel
Your foreman talkgroup.
Critical lift
75%+ of chart capacity, two or more cranes, personnel, over occupied structures, or an unusual load. Needs an LP- plan.
Deck
A level of a structure. Deck 0 is the ground. Below grade is Deck minus 1, and so on.
D-ring
The steel ring between your shoulder blades on a full-body harness. Your fall-arrest lanyard or SRL clips here.
Dunecrete
Alkaline sand-polymer binder. Burns wet skin. Cutting it makes silica dust.
Engulfment
Being swallowed by flowing sand, liquid or polymer.
Entrant
A worker cleared to go inside a confined space.
Entry supervisor
A foreman or general foreman with the HS-SA endorsement. Signs and ends PTW-CS.
Exclusion zone
An area roped off and kept clear: under overhead work and loads, around a crane's swing, on haul roads.
Fall clearance
The distance needed below your anchor to stop before you hit anything. 18.5 ft with a 6 ft shock-absorbing lanyard.
FerroLattice-7
Printed structural polymer, extruded at about 500°F.
Fire watch
A trained crew member in a red FIRE WATCH vest who watches hot work and stays 60 min after it (NFPA 51B). No rescue or command authority.
First aider
Trained in CPR and Stop the Bleed. One in every crew.
Foreman talkgroup
Radio talkgroup for crew check-ins and routine crew traffic, for example "C-F14".
Free fall
The distance you drop before your fall arrest starts to work. 6 ft max.
Grid · grid post
A numbered block, 1–40, inside a sector. The number is painted on every grid post.
Group lockout
Yellow job locks on every isolation point, keys in a yellow lockbox, and a personal lock from every worker on the box.
Group-lockout lead
The authorized employee named in charge of a group lockout.
Hasp
A clamp that holds several padlocks on one isolation point.
Hatch
An opening into a confined space, tank or hull void.
Heat index
The NWS "feels like" temperature in shade, from air temperature and humidity. Site heat levels use it up to 124°F, and air temperature from 125°F.
Heat levels
Normal, Caution, Extreme caution, Danger, Extreme, Extreme heat entry, Site closed (HS-03).
Heat ramp
Days 1–5 capped at 20/40/60/80/100% of the shift in heat, and half the table's work minutes on Days 1–4 (“No limit” = 30/30). Resets to Day 1 after fever, vomiting or diarrhea, any heat illness, or 7+ days away from heat work.
High-sail load
A wide, flat load that catches wind: wall panels, hull plate, roof sections.
Hot work
Welding, cutting, grinding, brazing or torch work. Needs a PTW-HW outside a welding bay.
The Hub
Site command, Central Med, Fire & Rescue HQ and the Atmospheric Office.
Hub Actual
Call sign of the Colony Construction Director in person. "Actual" means the commander, not the desk.
Hub Command
The site command desk at the Hub.
Hull void
The space between hab walls and decks. Often purged with inert gas.
Incident commander
The first person on scene, until relieved, who hands over command out loud. Fire & Rescue takes fire, rescue and hazmat scenes.
Inert gas
Argon or nitrogen. Pushes oxygen out. One breath can drop you.
Intrinsically safe
Built so it can't make a spark or get hot enough to ignite flammable gas. Required for lights and radios where flammables are possible.
Isocyanates
Chemicals in spray foams, polyurethane coatings and QuickSet C-4X. A leading cause of work-related asthma. They sensitize.
Isolation point
A disconnect, valve or blind where energy is shut off and locked.
Lanyard
The strap between your harness D-ring and the anchor. A shock-absorbing lanyard tears open to soften the stop.
Leading edge
The open edge of a deck or roof that moves as work goes on.
Lift Director
The Crane & Lift supervisor (white hat) who approves lift plans and directs critical lifts.
Lift lead
A qualified rigger (orange hat) named in the lift briefing who directs a routine lift. The Lift Director directs critical lifts.
Lift talkgroup
The radio talkgroup named on each lift plan, for example "Lift C-07". Ch 11 is for coordination only.
Location format
Sector-grid, structure, deck, landmark: "Charlie-14, Tower B, Deck 3, east stair."
Lockout · tagout
Every energy source isolated and held at zero under your own lock. A tag alone is only a warning.
Locks
Red: personal, engraved with your Colonist ID. Yellow: job lock for group lockout. Blue: Electrical & Power isolation. One person, one lock, one key.
Lone work
Working out of sight of others. None at Caution or higher, at height, in confined spaces or near wadis and trenches.
Magnetic field rig
A lifting magnet or field-forming rig. Lower the load, dump the coils, verify the field is at or below the rig's posted safe level with a gauss meter.
Man down
Emergency type for a collapsed or hurt person when no other type fits, including a burn with nothing still burning.
Microsleep
A few seconds of sleep, often with the eyes open. A fatigue warning sign.
Muster warden
A named crew lead at each muster point who collects headcounts and reports once to the Super.
Near miss
An event that could have hurt someone and didn't. Report it on HS-IR.
New waker
Slang for a newly revived worker. Every new waker starts on the heat ramp.
One lay
The length of wire rope in which one strand makes one full turn. Used to count broken wires.
Permit board
The board at every structure where every live permit is posted.
Permit-required confined space
A confined space with a dangerous atmosphere, engulfment, a trapping shape or another serious hazard. On this site every confined space counts as one (site rule) until the entry supervisor signs a written certificate at the hatch that every hazard has been removed (HS-SA).
Power cell
Site battery. Always live. It can't be drained.
Purge tag
A tag on a hatch meaning inert gas has been used inside.
Qualified electrical worker
Electrical & Power worker (blue hat). The only workers who test for voltage or work on power-cell terminals.
Qualified person
Old Earth term (OSHA 1926.32): someone who has proven, by degree, certificate or long experience, that they can solve problems in that subject. Qualified rigger, qualified signal person and qualified electrical worker are badge endorsements here.
QuickSet C-4X
Epoxy-isocyanate fast-cure resin. A sensitizer. A big mixed batch can run away and ignite.
RAMP band
Black-and-yellow chevron band reading "RAMP", with a reflective strip, worn front and back of the helmet on heat-ramp Days 1–5. RAMP isn't an acronym. It's "ramp" in capitals so it reads from a distance.
Revival Med Bay
The SRU's clearance bay: clearance, weigh-in, badge, radio, RAMP band, and re-clearance after illness or injury. Not for injuries.
Rhabdo
Rhabdomyolysis. Muscle breakdown after hard work in heat. Dark urine. It can wreck your kidneys.
Safety-critical role
A role where one slow reaction can kill someone else. Hour limits on HS-SD.
Sector
One of 8 site areas, Alpha to Hotel.
Sensitized
Your body has become allergic to a chemical such as an isocyanate or epoxy. Even a trace can set off asthma or a rash. It's permanent.
Shelter
Sealed, cooled shelter with a two-door airlock, in every grid block. Call sign "Shelter [Sector]-[grid]".
Shift weigh station
The scale at the cooling station. Weigh in and out. Pull off at 2% body weight lost.
Site rule
A DCW rule stricter than, or extra to, the Old Earth standards. Always labeled "(site rule)".
Sleep inertia
Grogginess after waking. Wait 15 minutes after a nap before safety-critical work.
Spotter
A trained worker who guides moving equipment from where the operator can see them.
Spray permit (PTW-SP)
Permit for spraying isocyanates or VeilCoat. Industrial Hygiene sets the respirator and spray zone; the area foreman signs (site rule). One location, one shift. See REF-PTW.
Stop-work authority
Anyone can stop unsafe work, with no penalty. Restart only when the crew lead confirms the fix with the person who stopped it.
Structure printer
Gantry machine that prints structures. Ch 12, call sign "Printer [Sector] n".
Suspension trauma
Blood pooling in the legs while you hang in a harness. Can make a conscious worker pass out in minutes.
Swing radius
The area a crane's turning body and counterweight sweep. Barricaded.
Tagline
A rope used to steer a load from outside its fall zone.
Talkgroup
A radio group for one team: a foreman talkgroup or a lift talkgroup.
Toe board
The board at the foot of a guardrail that stops tools being kicked off. 3.5 in minimum.
Triage colors
Green minor, yellow delayed, red immediate, black expectant (START). Never used for heat levels.
Type C soil
The least stable soil class. All site sand counts as Type C. Max slope 1.5:1 (34°).
VeilCoat
Nano-ceramic heat-shield spray with a flammable solvent.
Wadi
A dry stream channel that floods in minutes. Leave wadis, trenches and low cuts at a flood warning.
Watch · warning
Sandstorm watch: possible within 2 hours. Sandstorm warning: expected within 30 minutes. Land loads, get down, go to shelter.
Work face
The place where your crew is working.
Zero energy
Every energy source isolated, released and tested at zero before any part of you goes into a machine.

Stop-work authority

Every role on this sheet carries it. Anyone can stop unsafe work. No penalty, no questions. Stop it, make it safe, tell your crew lead (your foreman if there's no crew lead; any green hat if it isn't fixed). Work restarts only when the crew lead confirms the fix with the person who stopped it. Every stop is logged.
