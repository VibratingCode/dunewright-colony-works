Heat kills.

This world is hotter than anything your body remembers. Read every line before your first shift.

Just woke up?

Your heat tolerance did not survive stasis.

However many seasons you've worked in heat before, you are a new worker now. Your body has to rebuild its heat tolerance from zero over 7–14 days. Most heat deaths happen in a worker's first few days on a hot site. Report to Med Bay for a weigh-in and a hydration check before your first shift. Wear the orange RAMP sticker on your helmet until Day 5.

Heat stroke

Confused, slurring, stumbling, collapsed or seizing, hot to the touch. They may still be sweating. Don't wait to be sure.

Cool first.
Transport second.

Nearly everyone survives if their body temperature is brought under 104° within 30 minutes. Every minute you wait costs them.

  1. Ch 1: "Emergency, emergency, emergency. Heat casualty", with sector-grid and deck. The medic is coming to you.
  2. Get them out of the heat. Strip off suit, vest and helmet.
  3. Dunk them in the ice trough at the nearest cooling station, up to the neck, holding the head up.
  4. No trough? Soak them, cover them in ice-water towels, pack ice at the neck, armpits and groin, and fan hard.
  5. No drinks if they're confused. Stay with them and keep cooling until the medic takes over.

Know the signs

In yourself and in your crew

Heat rash

Clusters of red bumps on the neck, chest or groin.

Do: keep the skin dry, cool off, use powder.

Heat cramps

Painful spasms in the legs, arms or belly during heavy sweating.

Do: shade, water plus electrolytes. Report it if cramps last over an hour.

Fainting

Dizziness or blacking out after standing or getting up fast.

Do: sit or lie down somewhere cool and sip water. Tell your lead.

Heat exhaustion

Heavy sweat, clammy skin, headache, nausea, weakness, short temper, dark urine.

Do: pull off the line, cool down, sip water. Forward Aid Station if not better in an hour. Done for the day.

Rhabdo

Muscle pain and weakness, urine dark like tea or cola after hard work.

Do: stop work and go to the Forward Aid Station now. It can wreck your kidneys.

Heat stroke

Confusion, slurred speech, collapse, seizures, body at 104° or above.

Do: follow the red box. Cool them on the spot.

Work / rest by WBGT

WBGT (wet-bulb globe temperature) is the heat-stress number your crew lead reads off the site meter. Unlike plain air temperature, it counts everything that heats your body: air temperature, humidity, direct sun and wind. That's why a humid 88° WBGT can be more dangerous than a dry 110° air reading.

Each cell shows minutes of work / minutes of rest per hour, plus water per hour. These times are for workers who have finished the 5-day post-stasis ramp. On Days 1–4 of your ramp, work half these times.

On the build

  • Metal burns. Sun-baked steel, rebar and hull plate pass 158°. Gloves on every time you touch metal.
  • Pours and lifts go at night. Concrete, welding and heavy rigging get scheduled for the cool hours.
  • Enclosed modules trap heat. Unfinished habs and hull sections can run far hotter than outside. Check the reading before you go in.
  • Cover up. Loose, light-colored, full-coverage clothing, a brimmed hard-hat shade, goggles. Bare skin soaks up heat when the air is hotter than you.
  • Wet cloth works in dry air. A soaked neck wrap cools you through evaporation. It stops working when it's humid.
  • Never sit on bare sand or deck plate. Use your mat.

Crew leads & construction managers

  • Take the WBGT reading at muster and every hour after. Post the level and the work/rest cycle.
  • Assign a buddy pair to every worker. Check every crew member at least every 30 min.
  • Keep water, shade and a cooling station no more than a short walk from any work face. Stock an ice trough on every deck.
  • Track each new waker's ramp day. No one skips the ramp because the schedule slipped.
  • Run a radio check every 30 min with each crew. A crew that misses one gets checked in person.
  • Brief the heat casualty plan at every muster: nearest trough, Ch 14 medical, evac route.
  • Plan heavy tasks for the night shift. Cut scope before you cut rest.

Stop-work authority

Every worker on this site can stop work when a crew member shows heat symptoms or water, shade or cooling isn't available. No penalty, no questions. A late habitat is better than a dead crew.
